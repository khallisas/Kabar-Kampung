Kampung Rukun Cilik

A “little village of harmony” for TK Negeri 2 Yogyakarta: four village traditions that help children name their feelings, calm down with an adult, notice kindness in friends, and carry it all home.

Setting: the classroom

  • A familiar setting for practising emotional regulation.
  • We observed disappointment, arguments, and competition among children.
  • Daily practice happens through routines, group activities, and turn-taking.
  • Teachers already guide children in managing emotions and frustration.
  • An important stage for developing emotional and social skills.
02The problem

The biggest concerns.

Two challenges came up in our observations and in conversations with the teachers.

1

Emotional regulation

Teachers said children cannot yet manage their emotions, and we saw anger come out as fighting and grabbing.

2

Parents’ communication and different parenting styles

Values taught at school fade when home does not continue them, and many families have two working parents.

04Intervention design

Kampung Rukun Cilik.

Kampung Rukun Cilik (“little village of harmony”) turns each class into a small Javanese village. Four village traditions teach children to name their feelings, calm down with an adult, notice kindness in friends, and carry all of it home. Every tradition at school has a family version at home, so what children learn in class continues after the bell.

05Theory mechanism

Why it should work.

Ecological systems & educational philosophy

Bronfenbrenner · Dewantara

School↔Home

Through Bronfenbrenner’s ecological systems theory, the intervention bridges the school–home divide by giving every school tradition a family version, so learning continues after the bell. The teacher embodies the pamong, teaching ing ngarsa sung tuladha (Dewantara’s philosophy: leading by example from the front) to give a direct, observable example.

Emotion generation & social learning

Gross · Gottman · Bandura

Notice→Name the feeling→Calm down

Gross’s process model and Gottman’s emotion coaching both start from the idea that recognising and naming a feeling is the first step before a child can calm down. Bandura’s social learning theory adds that children learn emotional regulation mainly by hearing trusted adults do it, so the teacher models this vocabulary every day.

Sociocultural scaffolding & response modulation

Vygotsky · Gross

Teacher-led→Short prompt→On their own

Vygotsky’s sociocultural theory (co-regulation): children aged 4–6 cannot yet calm down alone in a full outburst, so they first regulate with an adult to build the capacity for self-soothing. In practice, Panggonan Tenang intercepts the emotion-generation process by changing the situation, moving attention, or calming the body.

Operant conditioning & vicarious reinforcement

Skinner · Bandura

Kind act→Specific praise→Repeated, and copied

Skinner: behaviour that is rewarded is repeated, so a daily routine explicitly praises waiting, helping, and kindness. Bandura’s vicarious learning: children copy what they see praised in a friend, and a cooperative, shared praise routine supports kindergarteners’ sharing.

06Parents as partners

Three ways parents take part.

Step 1Audience

Parents receive information and reports about their children’s behaviour from the school.

Step 2Client

Parents receive training and services from the school on how to carry out the programme at home.

Step 3Partner

Parents evaluate, contribute to the programme, and reinforce the character traits at home.

Partnerships help

Parent–school partnerships improve social and behavioural development (Smith et al., 2020).

Whole-school

A whole-school approach is more comprehensive (Goldberg et al., 2019).

Home reinforces

Parents reinforce learning at home, through Layang Misi and the weekly Kabar Kampung.

08Evaluation

What to consider, before to after.

BeforeWeek −2 to 0

Are we ready to measure?

  • Pilot the cards and stories with 2–3 children outside the sample: clear pictures? Javanese or Bahasa?
  • Train the observer: two raters score the same 3 children to check agreement.
  • Start the incident tally 2 weeks before the traditions begin.
  • Pre-test every child (week 0).
DuringWeek 1 to 16

Is it running as planned?

  • Fidelity: a weekly tick sheet per teacher (did it run? was the script used?).
  • Dosage: days each tradition ran; Layang Misi return rate.
  • Weekly incident tally and calm-down log.
  • Watch side effects: the corner used to avoid tasks, the same children getting thin praise.
  • A weekly 5-minute check-in is used to adjust.
AfterWeek 16 to 20

Did children change, and does it last?

  • Post-test with the same tools, observer, room, and language.
  • Feedback from teachers, parents (WhatsApp poll), and children (smiley scale).
  • Follow-up about 4 weeks later, without our support.
  • Report back to the school and parents; agree on what continues.

Pre- and post-test instruments

The same instruments are used before and after the intervention to measure the difference.

What to keep in mind

Children grow anyway

Ages 4–6 develop fast, so a pre–post gain cannot be fully credited to the programme. Option: one class starts a few weeks later as a waitlist, so every class still gets the programme.

Rater bias

Teachers may rate their own class kindly, so the child tasks are done by an outside observer, and teacher and parent ratings are cross-checked.

Child-friendly testing

Play-like, one child at a time. A child can say no or stop at any time and is never pushed.

Timing

Pre and post at a similar time of day. Avoid testing right after holidays or big events such as PORSENITAS.
